Hi, this is Andrea.

This June I attended HPSR2026 (High Performance Switching and Routing) 
in Montreal, Canada. It was a really great experience in the foggy and 
cloudy Montreal, the Gothic style mixed with ~finance bros~ skyscrapers 
made it a peculiar contrast; in the meantime I also visited a friend of 
mine doing his Master's thesis at the University of Montreal.

![montreal](montreal.JPG)

I was surprised by how good the conference turned out to be,
mainly due to the ~banquets~ fact that
the conference followed a single-track
format. Basically I didn't have to choose between different parallel sessions,
and had to attend every presentation. This made it way easier for me to
discover new topics, perspectives and get to know very, *very* interesting people.
You might expect a telco conference about
switches and routers to have little in common with hot topics like AI.
Well, that could be not further from the truth: AI ~devastated this field as well~
I'm joking.

Instead, I discovered the beauty of 
[DCNs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_center_network_architectures),
(for example always seen optical switches as something too far from my interests, these
words didn't have a shape before), more low level topics (what I am mostly
thrilled about)
such as [RoCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDMA_over_Converged_Ethernet),
source routing in Linux, and more infrastructure level problems, such as how network
architectures are becoming increasingly aware of GenAI models and tasks.
As a pratical example think of serving [MoE architectures](https://huggingface.co/blog/moe)
by physically locating different experts on different machines of the same slice,
and now the router component actually becomes a packet router.
This has been my best experience at a conference so far.

![tag](hpsr-tag.jpg)

Finally, I am realizing that even though everyone today is pushing people
in thinking that you want to ~build a startup and do a millionaire exit~
make tons of money to be happy,
(In SF this is the actual mentality, @ Stanford it's even more predominant),
I might want to do a PhD ~but not in Italy, please God~, 
and ultimately *play* with Big-Tech *toys*,
solving real-world, thrilling problems, that affect millions or even billions
of people.

> because let's be real, big-tech is the only guy with the big toys,
> and you might want to play with them

p.s. the work I presented is [Federated Intrusion Detection with Key-Value
Cached Transformers in 5G RANs](static/2026_HPSR_DiMatteo.pdf) with Emilio
Paolini, Prof. Valcarenghi and Prof. Andriolli.
